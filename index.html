Elizabeth W. Eisenhauer's cross-disciplinary research directions

I'm a statistician at Westat, working at the intersection of causal inference, survey research, program evaluation, and data science. The areas below are directions I've been developing through webinars, talks, and writing, and that I'm hoping to build upon.

Statistician & Evaluator Collaboration

My interest in connecting statisticians and evaluators began with a 2023 evaluation of a community engagement program, followed by an introductory lecture and invited panel at JSM 2024, and continued through an Amstat News description of the panel’s key debates. That work led to the ASA President’s 2025 Evidence to Impact Network, a collaboration with the American Evaluation Association’s Quantitative Methods Topical Interest Group.

  • webinar The Evidence Partnership: Bridging Statistical Thinking and Evaluation Practice, organized July 2025, Evidence to Impact Network
  • article Panelists Discuss How Statisticians, Evaluators Turn Evidence into Impact, authored March 2025, Amstat News
  • slides Introductory Overview Lecture: Program Evaluation, co-presented August 2024, Joint Statistical Meetings
  • slides A Retrospective Evaluation of a COVID-era Community Engagement Program, presented August 2023, Joint Statistical Meetings

Study Design & Sample Size Determination

My interest in sample size justification is about the statistical argument behind funding data and evidence-generation activities. I’m interested in the implications of those arguments, and in how statisticians can be better stewards of our role early in the design of studies.

  • comment ASA Response to NIH Request for Information: Measuring and Rewarding Scientific Impact (Section 1.3 Adequacy at the design stage: making evidentiary expectations auditable), co-wrote
  • webinar The Power of Power: Elevating Power Analysis as a Tool for Credible, Fundable, and Impactful Research, organized August 2025, Royal Statistical Society Emerging Applications Section
  • slides Sample Size Determination in Survey Design: Balancing Cost, Precision, and Bias, presented February 2026, AAPOR Survey Costs Workshop
🪶

Statistics in the Field: Evaluating Conservation Programs

Not all data collection happens in a lab or a survey; some of it happens on farms in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, checking nest boxes for kestrel eggs. Working with my dad on an American Kestrel conservation program got me thinking about how we make evaluation decisions in the field: what to measure, what counts as success, and how to balance statistical rigor with the realities of working in nature. I'm interested in what citizen science and ecological monitoring can teach statisticians about study design under constraints.

  • report Summary of an American Kestrel Nest Box Program in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, co-authored Steve C. Eisenhauer & Elizabeth W. Eisenhauer · Pennsylvania Birds, 2022

Inspired by History

The history of statistics is full of people who were rigorous, courageous, and largely forgotten, and I think there's a lot we can learn from them about what statistical practice can and should be. I'm interested in how historical figures and moments can inform how we think about methodology, professional norms, and how best to shape the discipline.

  • webinar Kate Claghorn: Progressive Era Statistician, First Woman ASA Fellow, organized May 2026, International Statistical Institute Committee on Women in Statistics & Caucus for Women in Statistics and Data Science & American Statistical Association History of Statistics Special Interest Group
🌐

Combining Evidence

I’m interested in how different forms of evidence can be combined to support more credible decision making. I organized an invited session on integrating mixed-methods evidence, causal knowledge, expert elicitation, and Bayesian priors, and presented work on using survey and serology data from blood donors to help monitor respiratory viruses in the U.S. population.

  • poster Sampling and Weighting Strategies for Nonprobability Survey and Serology Data, presented June 2026, IMSI New Horizons on Model Transportability and Data Integration
  • session S404: Coordinating Evidence for Better Research Design: Priors, Assumptions, and Scientific Judgment, organized October 2026, IDWSDS 2026
